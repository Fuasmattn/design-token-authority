# Spec: Landing page redesign — one story, quietly told

**Status:** approved (2026-10-17, after seven mockup rounds with the maintainer); **revised 2026-10-18** and **2026-10-19** (decision 12) after two prototype rounds on the key visual (decisions 3, 4 and 8–11 below)
**Depends on:** [docs-site](./docs-site.md) (site, tokens, design system), [agent-interop](./agent-interop.md) (the commands the page shows), [TICKET-090](../tickets/090-first-npm-release.md) (`npm create dta` works as written)
**Replaces:** the landing layout of TICKET-071/096/098/099 (hero + problem + how + workflow + agents + trust + cards)
**Reference mockups:** [`landing-redesign/`](./landing-redesign/) (throwaway HTML, literal colours; the look, not the code). **The accepted prototype** is [`landing-redesign/prototypes/p17-page.html`](./landing-redesign/prototypes/p17-page.html), generated by `gen16.py` (hero renderer in `gen15.py`); its controls (tilt, size, position) are prototype-only.

## Why

The current page lists things: three numbered problems, five numbered steps, three numbered points, three more. Restyling the lists did not help (five variants tried); the research behind the redesign ([Evil Martians, 100 dev-tool landing pages](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/we-studied-100-devtool-landing-pages-here-is-what-actually-works-in-2025)) ranks plain feature lists as the weakest way to present a tool and favours a problem-led story shown through the product itself. Two more findings shaped the result:

1. **Designers already see their change in Figma.** The page must never promise "see your change". The promise is: see what your change does to the product that already exists, and keep design and product in step. (See [concept-notes](./concept-notes.md), "The loop has two sides".)
2. **The abstraction was the problem, not the palette.** The diff card (three blocks: baseline, candidate, diff) had to be decoded. A concrete component before and after, with the token named on it, needs no decoding.

## The page

Hero → the workflow strip (decision 12) → one change in four scenes → facts → final call to action. Everything else goes.

1. **Hero.** Headline: _The deterministic centre of gravity for design token workflows, built for humans and **agents**._ ("agents." in the action colour, italic; the type-cycling word is gone.) Subline: _A toolset to lint, structure-check, transform and visually diff design tokens. Synced with Figma, shipped as pull requests._ Two actions: `npm create dta` as a **command chip** (decision 9) and "Read the quick start" (outline). Behind the whole hero, the **key visual** (decision 3): the gravity well, with cubes, spheres and dust orbiting it; the copy sits on the left, the well to the right (desktop) or above the copy (phone), and the mesh fades out before the first scene. There is no separate loop section.
2. **One change, four scenes.** Each scene: a numbered kicker, one heading, one short paragraph, the command as a command chip, and one example embedded in the surface (no border, no card chrome). Examples are **Orbit Burger** (decision 2).
   - 01 _Something changes_ — "A change lands as a pull request." Three token rows (surface, brand colour, card radius) with the two edited rows outlined in the action colour, dashed. `dta pull`.
   - 02 _The checks run first_ — "Structure and names are checked before anyone looks." Checks in the strip's order (lint passed, build passed, diff changes to review · 2 stories). `dta lint`. (Revised 2026-10-19: was the diff scene.)
   - 03 _A person decides_ — "Approve by token, not by screenshot." **One component, two states:** the Orbit Burger "Launch order" button as _main_ and _this change_ (radius 8 → 24, obvious; brand colour Nova 500 → 600, easy to miss), two lines underneath naming the token behind each share of the differences, then the diff check waiting for a person and the approve action under it. No redlines, no card. `dta diff`. (Revised 2026-10-19: the diff and the decision are one scene; was the checks scene.)
   - 04 _Your agent uses the same commands_ — "No hard-coded values, even from agents." Real `dta token find` and `dta audit` output. `dta skill add`.
3. **Facts.** Four big words, each under a thin rule, with one muted line beneath; no glyphs, no boxes: Open source; Your structure; Every Figma plan; Deterministic. Four columns on desktop, two on tablet, a list on phones.
4. **Final call to action.** "Start with one command." and the `npm create dta` command chip, centred. Around it a few objects (two cubes, two spheres, four motes) float free with a slight scroll parallax: nothing holds them yet (decision 10). No dark band, no second well: the page stays on one surface.

Nothing numbered beyond the scene kickers. No FAQ, no cards, no "why trust it", no bento.

## Decisions

1. **Quiet surface.** One background. Examples sit on it as soft shapes (`--bg-surface`) without borders; dark objects are only the terminal and the Orbit Burger cards. White space roughly doubles against the current page (`--space-36` between scenes).

2. **Orbit Burger** is the example product: a space burger delivery app. Deep-violet surfaces, a mint accent, "Nova" orange for the brand colour, a CSS-drawn burger with dashed orbit rings, copy such as "Saturn Smash · Double patty, ring sauce, zero-g pickles · Launch order · 12 min". The palette is deliberately not ours, so examples read as a customer's UI, never as part of the page. Per the site rule (no colour literals), its colours become an **example token collection** built with `dta build` like the rest (TICKET-105); and the example becomes a **real fixture with stories**, so the numbers on the page come from a real `dta diff` run, not from copy.

3. **The key visual is a gravity well, and it is decoration.** (Revised 2026-10-18; replaces the two-lane path. The path and the workbench stay in the reference folder as rejected.) The hero background is one polar mesh — spokes and log-spaced rings — lifted onto the surface z(r) = −K(1/r − 1/R_far), seen tilted (64°) and with perspective, so the surface narrows to a vanishing point below the mouth: the funnel to infinity is the surface itself. The rings flow inward at constant speed in log r; the spokes are logarithmic spirals that slowly turn, so the mesh swirls into the hole. The surface is painted opaque, back to front, so the near wall hides the far wall (no see-through tube); the lines darken toward the throat (`well.throat`) and the mouth is a small ellipse in the other theme's ground with a glowing rim. Outside the well the view blends back to a flat plane before the mesh fades at the screen edges, so 3D hands over to the page; a wash of the page colour fades the top. Around the well, on two dashed orbit rings (outer in the subtle colour, inner in the action colour): small 3D objects — cubes in orange, blue and ink, shaded spheres, dust — orbiting slowly in the plane of the surface. The visual tells no story and labels nothing; the headline and the scenes do that. "Everything gravitates towards the token" is what it means, and that is enough. Defaults from the prototype: tilt 64°, size S, position A, x offset +8 %, y 0; one spoke turn in 120 s, ring speed 0.085 in log r per second.

4. **Motion.** The mesh flows and the objects orbit continuously; a slight parallax on scroll (mesh 0.35, objects 0.18) and, at the end of the page, the free objects at their own depths (0.12–0.4). `prefers-reduced-motion`: one still frame of the mesh, objects at rest, no parallax. Tapping the well pauses it (prototype; optional on the site). Canvas work is capped at 2× device pixel ratio and is purely decorative (`aria-hidden`).

5. **Honesty rule, unchanged.** Every command on the page exists (`landing.test.ts` checks against `COMMAND_NAMES`); `npm create dta` is shown unmarked by maintainer decision (098); outputs shown as real are real (the agent scene; the diff shares, from a recorded `dta diff` run in `site/src/landing/diff-run.json`, TICKET-111); illustrative text is labelled.

6. **Design system first.** New UI becomes components under `site/src/components/ds` with a docs page and stories before the page uses them (CLAUDE.md rule): `Scene`, `ExampleButton` (Orbit Burger, two states), `TokenRows`, `Checks`, `Facts`, `CommandChip` (the copyable command; `CommandButton` is retired or becomes it), `GravityWell` and `FloatingObjects`. The current `DiffCard` is retired from the landing page (kept for the design-system docs until the diff report moves, TICKET-109).

7. **The diff report follows.** The before/after presentation (rendered story with the changed region outlined in signal red and the token callout on the change) replaces the three-thumbnail row in the `dta diff` report in a later ticket (TICKET-109); the landing page does not wait for it.

8. **One surface, no grid.** (Revised 2026-10-18.) The drafting-table grid paper leaves the landing page entirely — body and hero band — and the design system's principle 4 is rewritten: the mesh of the well is the only "paper you can measure on"; everything else is one plain surface. The site's docs pages and the diff report keep `bg-grid` only where something is literally being inspected (previews); the landing page uses it nowhere. Borders, not shadows, stays (the shadow prototype was tried and rejected for accessibility: 3:1 edges, forced colours).

9. **Commands are chips you can copy.** Every command on the page — the hero's `npm create dta`, the four scene commands, the final call — is the same component: a `$` prompt in the terminal prompt colour, the command in mono, a copy icon at the end that becomes a tick for 1.6 s after copying; terminal colours (ink on cream, cream on ink in the dark theme); the hero's one step larger. The primary call to action is therefore not blue: the one blue surface on the page is the approve action of scene 03. The blinking caret is gone.

10. **The end mirrors the beginning without repeating it.** The final section has no second well (tried, too much); a few objects float free at different scroll depths, the point being that nothing holds them yet, and the command chip is what the hero's well was: the centre.

11. **AAA.** Every text pair on the page reaches 7:1 (4.5:1 for large text) in both themes, measured over the effective background (opacity and nesting included). Consequences for the design system (TICKET-110): `fg.muted` darkens in the light theme (8:1 on the canvas); step labels, "this change" and the approve action use `action.text` (9.7:1 light, 8:1 dark) rather than `action.bg`; the terminal's dim lines are at 0.78 opacity, its prompt and success colours a step lighter; the Orbit Burger button is large text (19 px bold) because its own brand orange cannot carry 7:1 at body size and the "easy to miss" darker orange has to stay what it is. A contrast audit over the rendered page becomes a site test (TICKET-110), so it cannot regress.

12. **The workflow at a glance (2026-10-19, maintainer).** Below the hero, one unnumbered strip shows the workflow as two sides meeting in a pull request: Change (Figma, token file or agent) → in the pull request: Check (`dta lint`) → Build (`dta build`) → Diff (`dta diff`) → Decide, and a fix goes back to whichever side caused it. It is the "How it works" anchor. The four scenes stay and follow the strip's order; the checks scene shows `dta lint` instead of `dta build` (scene order revised the same day, see the revision note of 2026-10-19). "Nothing numbered beyond the scene kickers" still holds. TICKET-112.

## Revision note (2026-10-25): the strip is a numbered list grouped by place

Maintainer decision on TICKET-153, after three drafts on the Design canvas: the workflow section (decision 12) is rebuilt as `WorkflowList`, one numbered row per step with the command on the label's baseline, grouped by where it happens: the change, the crossing (`dta pull`), and the pull request as one card. It is numbered and it is a card, so "nothing numbered beyond the scene kickers" and "no cards" no longer hold for this one section; the four scenes and the rest of the page are unchanged. The design system carries the rules (component _WorkflowList_, principle 6 "One rhythm per list").

## Revision note (2026-10-19): scenes follow the strip

Maintainer decision on TICKET-114 item 5: scenes 02 and 03 swap, so the scene commands read in the strip's order (`dta pull` → `dta lint` → `dta diff` → `dta skill add`). Scene 02 is now the checks (lint, then every output rebuilt; nothing there needs a person), scene 03 the differences together with the decision: the approve action moved from the checks scene to the diff scene, under the one check that waits for a person (`dta diff` · changes to review), because what a person approves is the differences, by token. Kickers, headings and copy were rewritten to read in that order; the one blue surface on the page (decision 9) is still the approve action of scene 03. `landing.test.ts` checks the order against the strip.

## Revision note (2026-10-18)

Two prototype rounds after approval (the handoff of the first is folded in here) replaced decisions 3 and 4 and added 8–11: the loop section is gone, the hero carries a gravity-well key visual as pure decoration, the page lost its grid, commands became copyable chips, the facts became four big words, the agent headline was rewritten and every text pair is AAA. Tickets 106, 107 and 108 were rewritten to match and TICKET-110 (design-system update) was added in front of them. The accepted prototype is `landing-redesign/prototypes/p17-page.html`.
